What do you do when money is at stake and the data hasn’t arrived yet?

I’ve been working on that question for nine years at Space307, a fintech company: first as a quant, then in monetization, now running product. Every answer I’ve found is a trade-off; the work is putting a price on it.

Confidence is a commodity: a test buys it with traffic and weeks of waiting. A wrong guess costs whatever it breaks.

This year AI has made most changes a week’s work and easy to undo, so a guess often costs less than a test. We stopped waiting for the data and got it wrong in both directions. Some careful changes could have just shipped; some fast ones broke more than we expected and had to be rolled back.

Where a wrong guess is hard to undo, it costs more than a test. That’s where we still buy the confidence.

Three things I still believe

Every call has one owner.

When the data won’t arrive in time, the owner decides how much confidence to buy first, then makes the call and answers for it.

Guesses ship with a label.

Acting on expert opinion is expected. Honesty means not weighing what we judged by eye the same as what we measured.

Models narrow the search.

Good ones show where to look. None tells you what to do. Better input doesn’t make the person who decides any less responsible.

The path

2017–2022

Quant Analyst

I started on the risk management side, pricing and hedging the derivatives our clients trade, from modeling how bad a bad day can get to mitigating it.

2022–2025

Monetization Lead

At the time, money questions belonged to every team, and so to no one. They became my job, and I built the team that handles them today.

Later I joined the management team and helped it agree on how much risk the company is willing to carry, for how long, and at what cost.

2026–now

Chief Product Officer

Today I run product and sit on the management team.

A guess: we skipped a refactoring estimated at a year for a cheap two-month rewrite. After launch almost nothing broke, so the bet was worth it.

A test: with A/B tests we caught two changes to how new traders make their first deposit, each about 5% worse on conversion, and we avoided both.

Career in detail

Happy to argue about

What trading is, and how people respond to uncertainty

An airline site shows a chart of ticket prices and tells you to buy before they rise. Do you need a button that pays when they fall, and why?

How far a product should follow what users choose

Offer 50× or 500× leverage and most traders will take the risk. It’s a strategic call: are you selling a financial instrument or a dopamine loop?

Which features earn money and which only move it around

Unless you know what you’re doing, steering users from one feature to another doesn’t automatically make them spend more.

When to stop tuning the money model

The daily work is tuning how the money gets made. The rarer call is reading where regulation and the market are heading, and seeing when tuning no longer pays and the model itself has to change.

Still open

How to make a company decide faster without making it harder on the people in it.

Working with me

Some calls are yours and some are mine. On yours, I’ll say so if I disagree, then commit to what you decide. On mine, I’ll ask the same of you.

When priorities collide or you can’t tell which way is right, bring it to me. We’ll talk it through, and if it’s still split, I’ll make the call.

If the people around me keep pulling in different directions, I haven’t explained the goal well enough.

Push back. Tell me plainly when an idea of mine is bad, and say it again if I don’t listen. Then help me find a better one: the problem still needs solving.
