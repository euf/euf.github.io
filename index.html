01

Profile

I’m a product executive who came up on the business side of fintech. Over eight years at Space307 — a trading platform serving emerging markets across Asia and the Middle East — I priced the risk and built the monetization function before I ran product. It taught me to read a product through its economics, and to respect how hard the craft underneath it is.

My strongest work is the org, not the roadmap. I rebuilt how my department decides — quicker, cheaper, evidence over opinion — and helped the company get deliberate about which bets earn rigor and which earn speed. When a call has to be made before the data is clean, I make it and own it.

I shaped the strategy the company runs on, and I lead by winning over people who don’t report to me. The work I’m proudest of is the people who’ve outgrown the roles I gave them.

I don’t confuse motion with progress. I’d rather name a hard problem early and plainly than smooth over how it looks.

04

How I lead

Management is the start of the hard part.

The best engineer gets made a lead and assumes the worst is behind them. It’s the opposite. I promote for the appetite to lead people over the best individual scorecard, and I’ve watched quiet ICs become the leads no one predicted.

The constraint is never where it was last quarter.

I moved a product org off measuring everything toward fast, cheap, evidence-first bets — and when the bottleneck moved from shipping to deciding, I moved the org again.

The model is a tool, not a verdict.

My quant years taught me to read the numbers as evidence, not a ruling. The job is separating the bets that compound in the economics from the ones that just demo well.

The people calls are the ones to get right.

I try to make them early and honestly — treat the person leaving with as much care as the team staying, and own the decision plainly instead of hiding behind process.
