Profile

Eight years at Space307, a trading platform serving dozens of markets across Asia and the Middle East. I priced the risk and built the monetization function before I ran product — and I don’t underestimate the craft that sits on top of the numbers.

My best work is organizational. I rebuilt how my department decides: quicker, cheaper, evidence over rank. I reset how the wider company picks its bets: rigor where it’s measurable, judgment where it isn’t. When a call has to come before the data is clean, I make it and put my name on it.

I shaped the product strategy the company runs on, and most of what I lead I don’t manage — people follow the argument, not the title. I hand ownership early and back the people who take it — that’s the part of the job I most want to be judged on. I’ll name a problem early, in plain words, rather than let it sit behind a clean status report.

Point of view

A conviction I build and hire against: durability is an economics problem before it’s a feature problem. In a category under tightening scrutiny — regulation closing in, growth getting expensive — the products that last are the ones whose unit economics still hold once the easy wins are gone. I’d rather get the money model right than repaint the funnel. The discipline travels: it’s what any regulated consumer-finance business needs, not just this one.

Trajectory

2017–2022

Quant · Risk Management

I owned the pricing and risk end of the book: how every instrument we trade is priced and hedged. I grew from junior to senior quant, and the hardest live-market decisions landed on my desk.

2022–2025

Monetization Lead

Monetization wasn’t a function yet; it was scattered across teams, owned by no one. Leadership carved it out and asked me to build it, and I did — from scratch: instrument pricing, partner-network economics, retention, and the team of quants and analysts that runs it today. This is where I moved from doing the analysis to leading the people who do it, and where I first had a hand in company strategy.

2026 – Now

Chief Product Officer

I own product end to end: a matrixed org of around eighty — five cross-functional delivery streams, plus design, research, monetization, and a lean growth-experimentation function. I sit on the company’s management team. I inherited four sound streams and built on them rather than tearing them down: I redrew where each one’s ownership starts and stops, brought monetization into the department with me, and stood up a fifth stream to find new business models. The bigger lever, though, was how the org decides: the operating model runs leaner for it, and every roadmap review now starts in the unit economics.

How I lead

The constraint is never where it was last quarter.

I moved a product org from measuring everything to backing fast, cheap, evidence-first bets. When the bottleneck moved from shipping to deciding, I moved it again.

The model is a tool, not a verdict.

My quant years taught me to read the numbers as evidence, not a ruling. The job is separating the bets that compound from the ones that just demo well.

The people calls are the ones to get right.

I make them early and honestly. I treat the person leaving with as much care as the team staying, and own the decision plainly instead of hiding behind process.

Know when to tune the engine and when to swap it.

Most of the work is tuning how the money gets made. The judgment that earns its keep is spotting the moment tuning stops paying and the engine itself has to change — early, from where regulation and the market are heading.
