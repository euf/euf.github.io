Profile

Eight years at Space307 — a trading platform for emerging markets across Asia and the Middle East — I priced the risk and built the monetization function before I ran product. It taught me to read a product through its economics, and to respect how hard the craft under it is.

My strongest work is the org. I rebuilt how my department decides: quicker, cheaper, evidence over opinion. I helped the wider company get deliberate about which bets earn rigor and which earn speed. When the call has to come before the data is clean, I make it and own it.

I shaped the strategy the company runs on, and I lead by winning over people who don’t report to me. The work I’m proudest of is the people who outgrew the roles I gave them. I don’t confuse motion with progress. I’d rather name a hard problem early than paper over it.

03

How I lead

The constraint is never where it was last quarter.

I moved a product org off measuring everything toward fast, cheap, evidence-first bets. When the bottleneck moved from shipping to deciding, I moved it again.

The model is a tool, not a verdict.

My quant years taught me to read the numbers as evidence, not a ruling. The job is separating the bets that compound in the economics from the ones that just demo well.

The people calls are the ones to get right.

I make them early and honestly. I treat the person leaving with as much care as the team staying, and own the call plainly instead of hiding behind process.

Think in business models, not features.

Repainting the funnel isn’t strategy. What matters is how the money is actually made, and where regulation is heading. That’s what tells you when to swap the engine and when to tune it.
