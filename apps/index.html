Apps

Weekend projects: apps I build for myself with AI agents. The agents write and test the code; I decide what each app is for, how it should feel, and when it’s good enough. None is released, so the user base is me. The apps are also the only product work I can show in full, so each comes with the decision that shaped it.

iOS & macOS

Mnemit

A flashcard app for iPhone and Mac, built for learning Greek vocabulary.

The hard part of a learning app is the same as in trading: getting someone to come back tomorrow. The spaced-repetition scheduler, FSRS, already knows which cards are due, so my part was the daily routine around those reviews. I kept the habit apart from the goal: a streak that costs two minutes, and a ring sized to your usual day, as in Apple Fitness.

Duolingo split its streak from the daily goal and kept more people coming back, though fewer reached the goal. I copied the split. A goal you keep missing wears motivation down, so I sized the ring from the review logs of 2,000 Anki users: the ring closes on about four days in five.

Rewards go to coming back and to words learned, never to cards answered. Friends compare words answered right this week, and I checked that grading your own answers too kindly can’t raise that number. Duolingo sells streak freezes to save a missed day. In Mnemit you repair a missed day by closing the next day’s ring, and there is nothing to buy.

FicTypeApp

A book reader for Mac and iPhone with its own typesetting engine.

I read my Russian books in it, on the Mac and the iPhone, so it had to set them the way printed books do: hyphenated, justified, with footnotes on the page that cites them (War and Peace has plenty). When the typographic canon and my eye disagreed about the margins, I looked at both on four page sizes and went with my eye.

macOS

Emdown

A native Mac editor for Markdown that shows formatted text and hides the markup.

Typora and Obsidian show the Markdown asterisks as soon as the cursor reaches a word. Emdown never does, and the file stays plain Markdown for whoever opens it next. I wrote down four user personas it’s for, from an art director to a grandmother who just wants TextEdit, before an agent was able to deliver real editing usability.

Tabench

A fast CSV editor for Mac that opens files with millions of rows.

With Tabench I had to decide what not to build. Tablecruncher, by Stefan Fischerländer, already opened CSV files that spreadsheets choke on, so I kept its engine, gave it a native Mac interface, and checked that every file comes out exactly as it went in.
