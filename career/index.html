Career

I joined Space307, a fintech company of 280+ people, as a quant analyst in 2017. Since January 2026 I’ve run product there. Below are the calls I made along the way, including the ones that didn’t work.

Decisions

Quant Analyst, Risk Management

2018

Did the heavy lifting on pricing: custom real-time models from the Black–Scholes–Merton family for the derivatives our clients trade.

2019

Designed an automated hedging system.

Wrote our market-risk policy, with every risk scenario mapped to its control.

2020

Set up enterprise risk management with little paperwork: risk owners in the teams, continuity plans that reuse what engineering already had.

Started the risk register and ran the first assessment of our top risks.

2021

Designed a novel financial instrument with trading experience in mind. Later my team turned part of it into a product update.

Split the risk team’s work into four areas and set the boundaries of mine: what I own, what we share, what belongs to others. My area, financial engineering, later became monetization.

Senior Quant Analyst, Monetization

2022

Took over the money questions no team owned, as my own area inside the risk department.

Started digging into why new traders leave so early: a cohort study, then an A/B test that limited their first trades. The first test failed; I worked out why and relaunched it.

Monetization Lead

2023

Became the lead of a new monetization line and hired its first quant, the start of the team that still runs it.

Focused my team on behavioral questions, including early churn: traders who leave within their first hours. Once we measured it, several of our explanations fell apart, such as the idea that users trade too aggressively. The metrics went into the company’s retention strategy.

2024

Joined the management team and co-wrote the company’s growth strategy, starting from risk appetite: how much risk the company is ready to carry when it chooses where to grow.

With a colleague, proposed treating projects that improve margin as an investment portfolio, with build costs kept apart from running costs. It became one of the strategy’s directions.

Stopped being the bottleneck for reward approvals and spread that expertise across my team.

2025

Co-wrote the company’s 2025 strategy, with retention as the product’s main goal.

My team designed several novel trading mechanics to strengthen our USP.

Moved monetization out of risk, towards product.

Chief Product Officer

Product is four functional teams (design, research, growth experimentation, monetization) and five delivery streams (cross-functional tiny teams). I sit on the management team and run product engineering with the CTO.

Dec 2025

Took over the year’s main product goal before becoming CPO, with one rule: no work goes into the plan without the barrier it removes.

Q1 2026

Gave each stream one key metric to lead, and named another stream to support it.

Set our 2026 product strategy and wrote down in advance what we’d do if any of its key bets failed.

Q2 2026

Pushed every team toward fast, untested changes.

Tied how much proof a change needs to what a mistake would cost. If it can be rolled back and the worst case is a month of weaker results, the owner can go by eye; payments and balances get tested.

Chose a two-month rewrite over a year of refactoring, and accepted that one notification flow broke, which we found only later.

Q3 2026

Widened that rule after some fast changes were rolled back: now anything that touches money gets measured.

Handed monetization to a successor from my team.

Set the rules for rebuilding product teams: when someone leaves, we redesign the work before we hire.

Q4 2026

Wrote product’s position for the 2027 strategy: every new bet gets a point where we either stop or put in more.
